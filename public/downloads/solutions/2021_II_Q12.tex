\textbf{(i)} Let $\ds d=1-p_A-p_B$

The match is won by $A$ after $r$ draws followed by an $A$ win.

\begin{align*}
P(A\text{ wins})
&=p_A+dp_A+d^2p_A+\cdots\\
&=p_A(1+d+d^2+\cdots)\\
&=\frac{p_A}{1-d}\\
&=\frac{p_A}{p_A+p_B}
\end{align*}

\bigskip\hrule\bigskip

\textbf{(ii)} After each game, the difference between the number of games won by $A$ and $B$ changes by $1$. The match ends when this difference is $2$ or $-2$.

First two games:

$$AA:\ p^2\Rightarrow A\text{ wins}$$

$$BB:\ q^2\Rightarrow B\text{ wins}$$

$$AB\text{ or }BA:\ 2pq\Rightarrow \text{back to }0$$

\begin{align*}
P_{A}&=p^2+2pqP_{A}\\
&=\frac{p^2}{1-2pq}\\
&=\frac{p^2}{p^2+q^2}
\end{align*}

\bigskip\hrule\bigskip

\textbf{(iii)}

\textbf{Cautious version}

Let $C_i$ be the probability of winning with $i$ tokens.

$$C_0=0,\  C_4=1$$

$$C_i=pC_{i+1}+qC_{i-1}\quad (1\leq i\leq3)$$

$$C_1=pC_2,\quad C_2=pC_3+qC_1,\quad C_3=p+qC_2$$


\begin{align*}
C_2&=p(p+qC_2)+qC_1\\
&=p^2+pqC_2+qC_1\\
&=p^2+pqC_2+pqC_2\\
&=\frac{p^2}{p^2+q^2}
\end{align*}

$$C_1=pC_2=\frac{p^3}{p^2+q^2}$$

\textbf{Bold version}

The player must double twice before losing all tokens.

$$B=p^2$$

Comparison,

\begin{align*}
C_1-B
&=\frac{p^3}{p^2+q^2}-p^2\\
&=\frac{p^2(p-p^2-q^2)}{p^2+q^2}\\
&=\frac{p^2q(p-q)}{p^2+q^2}
\end{align*}

$$p-q>0\iff p>\frac12$$

So cautious is more likely if $\ds \frac12<p<1$

bold is more likely if $\ds 0<p<\frac12$
