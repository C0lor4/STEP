\textbf{(ii)}

$$(x^2+2)\frac{\sin x}{x^3}=\frac{\sin x}{x}+\frac{2\sin x}{x^3}$$

$$\int \frac{2\sin x}{x^3}\,dx=-\frac{\sin x}{x^2}+\int \frac{\cos x}{x^2}\,dx$$

$$\int \frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx=-\frac{\cos x}{x}-\int \frac{\cos x}{x^2}\,dx$$

\begin{align*}
\int (x^2+2)\frac{\sin x}{x^3}\,dx
&=\int \frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx
-\frac{\sin x}{x^2}
-\frac{\cos x}{x}
-\int \frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx\\
&=-\frac{\sin x+x\cos x}{x^2}+C
\end{align*}

\textbf{(iii)(b)}

$$\int_a^{2a}\frac{e^x}{x}\,dx=\int_a^{2a}\frac{e^x}{x^2}\,dx$$

$$\because \frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{e^x}{x}\right)=\frac{e^x}{x}-\frac{e^x}{x^2}$$

$$\left[\frac{e^x}{x}\right]_a^{2a}=0$$

$$\frac{e^{2a}}{2a}-\frac{e^a}{a}=0$$

$$a=\ln2$$

\bigskip\hrule\bigskip

\textbf{(iii)(c)} Assume distinct integers $m,n$ exist and $n<m$.

$$\left[\frac{e^x}{x}\right]_m^n=0\implies\frac{e^n}{n}=\frac{e^m}{m}$$

From the graph in \textbf{(iii)(a)}, $\ds \frac {e^n}{n}$ must lie in $(0,1)$.

$$\therefore \text{Contradiction, no distinct integers }m,n\text{ exist}$$
