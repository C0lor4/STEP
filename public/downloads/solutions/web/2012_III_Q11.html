Let the horizontal plane containing the fixed point \(P\) be the zero potential energy reference level (with the downward direction as the positive \(y\)-axis). The rope is uniform with a linear mass density of \(\rho=\frac{2M}{2L}=\frac{M}{L}\).

1. Initial State (\(t=0\)):

  • Initial kinetic energy of the system: \(E_{k0}=0\)
  • Initial potential energy of the system (center of mass of the rope falls by \(\frac{L}{2}\)): \(E_{p0}=-2Mg\left(\frac{L}{2}\right)=-MgL\)
  • Total initial mechanical energy: \(E_0=-MgL\)

2. Final State (particle has fallen a distance \(x\)):

  • Final kinetic energy: The particle and the moving section of the rope have the same speed \(v\).
    \[ E_k=\frac12mv^2+\frac12\left[\frac{M}{L}\left(L-\frac{x}{2}\right)\right]v^2 =\frac12\left(m+M-\frac{Mx}{2L}\right)v^2 \]
  • Final potential energy: Composed of three parts: the particle, the stationary section of the rope (length \(L+\frac{x}{2}\)), and the moving section of the rope (length \(L-\frac{x}{2}\)).
    \[ E_p=-mgx -\underbrace{\frac{Mg}{L}\left(L+\frac{x}{2}\right)}_{\text{mass of stationary section}} \underbrace{\frac12\left(L+\frac{x}{2}\right)}_{\text{COM of stationary section}} -\underbrace{\frac{Mg}{L}\left(L-\frac{x}{2}\right)}_{\text{mass of moving section}} \underbrace{\left(\frac{L}{2}+\frac{3x}{4}\right)}_{\text{COM of moving section}} \]

    Expanding and simplifying yields:

    \[ E_p=-mgx-MgL-Mgx+\frac{Mgx^2}{4L} \]

3. Conservation of Mechanical Energy (\(E_0=E_k+E_p\)):

\[ -MgL=\frac12\left(m+M-\frac{Mx}{2L}\right)v^2 -mgx-MgL-Mgx+\frac{Mgx^2}{4L} \]
\[ \frac12\left(\frac{mL+ML-\frac12Mx}{L}\right)v^2 =gx(m+M)-\frac{Mgx^2}{4L} \]

Extracting the common factor and rearranging proves the expression:

\[ v^2=\frac{2gx\left(mL+ML-\frac14Mx\right)} {mL+ML-\frac12Mx} \]

Using the kinematic differential relationship \(a=v\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac12\frac{d(v^2)}{dx}\), we differentiate the above expression for \(v^2\).

Let \(v^2=\frac{u}{w}\), where:

  • Numerator \(u=2gL(m+M)x-\frac12Mgx^2\implies u'=2gL(m+M)-Mgx=2gw\)
  • Denominator \(w=L(m+M)-\frac12Mx\implies w'=-\frac12M\)

According to the quotient rule:

\[ \frac{d(v^2)}{dx} =\frac{u'w-uw'}{w^2} =\frac{(2gw)w-u\left(-\frac12M\right)}{w^2} =2g+\frac{Mu}{2w^2} \]

Substituting into \(a=\frac12\frac{d(v^2)}{dx}\):

\[ a=g+\frac{Mu}{4w^2} \]

Substituting the original expressions for \(u\) and \(w\) back and simplifying proves the expression:

\[ a=g+\frac{Mgx\left(mL+ML-\frac14Mx\right)} {2\left(mL+ML-\frac12Mx\right)^2} \]

Observe the expression for acceleration:

\[ a=g+\frac{Mgx\left(mL+ML-\frac14Mx\right)} {2\left(mL+ML-\frac12Mx\right)^2} \]

At any instant after the particle is released (\(x>0\)):

  1. Denominator: \(2\left(mL+ML-\frac12Mx\right)^2\) is a perfect square and is always strictly positive (\(>0\)).
  2. Numerator: The problem states that \(x<2L\), therefore \(\frac14Mx<\frac14M(2L)=\frac12ML\). From this, we can deduce the term inside the parenthesis:
    \[ \left(mL+ML-\frac14Mx\right) >\left(mL+ML-\frac12ML\right) =mL+\frac12ML>0 \]

Since \(M,g,x\) are all positive numbers, and the term inside the parenthesis is also positive, the entire fractional term is strictly greater than \(0\). Therefore:

\[ a=g+(\text{positive number})>g \]

The original proposition is proven.
