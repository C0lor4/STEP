(i) Let \(\displaystyle u=2-x,\ du=-dx\)

\[ \begin{aligned} \int \ln(2-x)\,dx &=-\int \ln u\,du\\ &=-(u\ln u-u)+C\\ &=-(2-x)\ln(2-x)+(2-x)+C \end{aligned} \]

(ii)

\[ y=\ln|x^2-4| \]
\[ |x^2-4|=1\implies x=\pm\sqrt3,\ \pm\sqrt5 \]

Vertical asymptotes: \(\displaystyle x=\pm2\)

(iii) In the first quadrant,

\[ y=\ln(4-x^2) \]

The curve meets the positive \(x\)-axis when

\[ \ln(4-x^2)=0\implies x=\sqrt3 \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Area} &=\int_0^{\sqrt3}\ln(4-x^2)\,dx\\ &=\Big[x\ln(4-x^2)\Big]_0^{\sqrt3} +2\int_0^{\sqrt3}\frac{x^2}{4-x^2}\,dx\\ &=2\int_0^{\sqrt3}\left(-1+\frac4{4-x^2}\right)\,dx\\ &=\left[-2x+2\ln\left(\frac{2+x}{2-x}\right)\right]_0^{\sqrt3}\\ &=-2\sqrt3+2\ln\left(\frac{2+\sqrt3}{2-\sqrt3}\right)\\ &=-2\sqrt3+4\ln\left(2+\sqrt3\right) \end{aligned} \]

(iv)

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_0^2 \ln(4-x^2)\,dx &=\Big[-(2-x)\ln(2-x)+(2+x)\ln(2+x)-2x\Big]_0^2\\ &=4\ln4-4 \end{aligned} \]

\(\because \ln(4-x^2)\geq 0\) on \(\displaystyle 0\leq x\leq\sqrt3\) and \(\displaystyle \ln(4-x^2)<0\) on \(\displaystyle \sqrt3<x<2\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \therefore \text{Area} &=2\left(\int_0^{\sqrt3}\ln(4-x^2)\,dx-\int_{\sqrt3}^{2}\ln(4-x^2)\,dx\right)\\ &=2\left(2\int_0^{\sqrt3}\ln(4-x^2)\,dx-\int_0^2\ln(4-x^2)\,dx\right)\\ &=4\left(4\ln(2+\sqrt3)-2\sqrt3\right)-2(4\ln4-4)\\ &=8+16\ln(2+\sqrt3)-16\ln2-8\sqrt3 \end{aligned} \]
