(i)

\[ y=x^3-3qx-q(1+q) \]
\[ \frac{dy}{dx}=3x^2-3q=3(x^2-q) \]
\[ \frac{dy}{dx}=0\implies x=\pm\sqrt q \]

When \(\displaystyle x=-\sqrt q\),

\[ y=-q\sqrt q+3q\sqrt q-q-q^2=-q(\sqrt q-1)^2<0 \]

When \(\displaystyle x=\sqrt q\),

\[ y=q\sqrt q-3q\sqrt q-q-q^2=-q(\sqrt q+1)^2<0 \]

\(\because\) Both turning points are below the \(x\)-axis.

\[ x\to-\infty\implies y\to-\infty \]
\[ x\to\infty\implies y\to\infty \]
\[ \therefore \text{the curve crosses the }x\text{-axis once only} \]

(ii)

\[ x=u+\frac qu \]
\[ \begin{aligned} x^3-3qx-q(1+q)&=0\\ \left(u+\frac qu\right)^3-3q\left(u+\frac qu\right)&=q(1+q)\\ u^3+\frac{q^3}{u^3}&=q(1+q) \end{aligned} \]

Let \(\displaystyle t=u^3\).

\[ t^2-q(1+q)t+q^3=0 \]
\[ (t-q)(t-q^2)=0 \]
\[ u^3=q\quad \text{or}\quad u^3=q^2 \]
\[ u=\sqrt[3]{q}\quad \text{or}\quad u=\sqrt[3]{q^2} \]
\[ x=u+\frac qu=\sqrt[3]{q}+\sqrt[3]{q^2} \]

(iii)

\[ \alpha+\beta=p,\ \alpha\beta=q \]
\[ \alpha^3+\beta^3=(\alpha+\beta)^3-3\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta)=p^3-3qp \]
\[ \begin{aligned} (\alpha^2-\beta)(\beta^2-\alpha)&=0\\ \alpha^2\beta^2-(\alpha^3+\beta^3)+q&=0\\ q^2-(p^3-3qp)+q&=0\\ p^3-3qp-q(1+q)&=0 \end{aligned} \]

By (ii), the real root is

\[ p=\sqrt[3]{q}+\sqrt[3]{q^2} \]
