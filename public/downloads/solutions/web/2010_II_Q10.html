(i) Let \(v_A\) be the final speed of particle \(A\), and \(v_B\) be the final speed of particle \(B\).

Assume the particles are colliding elastically. By conservation of momentum and kinetic energy, we have:

\[ bmu = mv_A - bmv_B \]
\[ \frac{1}{2}bmu^2 = \frac{1}{2}bmv_B^2 + \frac{1}{2}mv_A^2 \]

Note: The equation \(bmu = mv_A - bmv_B\) implicitly defines \(v_B\) as the speed in the opposite direction of the initial velocity \(u\).

From equations above, we can simplify:

\[ bu = v_A - bv_B \implies b(u + v_B) = v_A \]
\[ bu^2 = bv_B^2 + v_A^2 \implies b(u^2 - v_B^2) = v_A^2 \]

From the second equation, we factorize to get \(b(u+v_B)(u-v_B)=v_A^2\). Substitute \(b(u+v_B)=v_A\) into this:

\[ v_A(u-v_B)=v_A^2 \implies u-v_B=v_A \]

Substitute \(v_A=u-v_B\) back into the momentum equation:

\[ \begin{aligned} bu &= (u-v_B)-bv_B\\ bu &= u-(1+b)v_B\\ (1+b)v_B &= u-bu\\ v_B &= \left(\frac{1-b}{1+b}\right)u \end{aligned} \]

Note: Since \(b>1\), \(v_B<0\). This implies that particle \(B\) actually continues to move in the same forward direction after the collision.

Now substitute \(v_B\) to find \(v_A\):

\[ \begin{aligned} v_A &= u-v_B\\ &= u-\left(\frac{1-b}{1+b}\right)u\\ &= \left(\frac{1+b-(1-b)}{1+b}\right)u\\ &= \left(\frac{2b}{1+b}\right)u \end{aligned} \]

Proof that \(v_A<2u\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{b}{1+b} &< 1\\ \implies 2\left(\frac{b}{1+b}\right) &< 2\\ \implies \left(\frac{2b}{1+b}\right)u &< 2u \quad \blacksquare \end{aligned} \]

(ii) From part (i), if a moving particle has mass \(b\) times that of a particle at rest, then the velocity of the resting object becomes \(\left(\frac{2b}{1+b}\right)u\).

Consider the first collision between \(B_1\) and \(B_2\). The masses are:

\[ \begin{aligned} m_{B_1} &= \lambda^{n+1-1}m = \lambda^n m\\ m_{B_2} &= \lambda^{n+1-2}m = \lambda^{n-1}m \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, \(m_{B_1}=\lambda m_{B_2}\). Applying the result from part (i) with \(b=\lambda\), the velocity of \(B_2\) is:

\[ v_{B_2}=\left(\frac{2\lambda}{1+\lambda}\right)u \]

This relationship applies to all subsequent collisions. For \(B_n\), the velocity is:

\[ v_{B_n}=\left(\frac{2\lambda}{1+\lambda}\right)^{n-1}u \]

Finally, \(B_n\) collides with particle \(A\) (mass \(m\)). Since \(m_{B_n}=\lambda^{n+1-n}m=\lambda m\), we apply the formula one last time:

\[ v_A=\left(\frac{2\lambda}{1+\lambda}\right)v_{B_n}=\left(\frac{2\lambda}{1+\lambda}\right)^n u \]

Since \(\lambda>1\), we have \(\frac{2\lambda}{1+\lambda}>1\). As \(n\to\infty\), \(\left(\frac{2\lambda}{1+\lambda}\right)^n\to\infty\), hence \(v_A\to\infty\). This proves there is no upper limit on the speed of \(A\).

Case \(\lambda=4\): We require \(v_A>20u\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \left(\frac{2\cdot4}{1+4}\right)^n u &> 20u\\ \left(\frac{8}{5}\right)^n &> 20 \end{aligned} \]

Taking \(\log_{10}\) on both sides:

\[ \begin{aligned} n\log_{10}\left(\frac{8}{5}\right) &> \log_{10}20\\ n &> \frac{\log_{10}2+1}{3\log_{10}2-(1-\log_{10}2)}\\ n &> \frac{\log_{10}2+1}{4\log_{10}2-1} \end{aligned} \]

Using \(\log_{10}2\approx0.30103\):

\[ n>\frac{1.30103}{1.20412-1}\approx6.37 \]

Thus, the least integer value is \(\min(n)=7\)
