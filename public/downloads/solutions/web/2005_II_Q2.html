(a)(ii)

Let \(\quad\displaystyle N=p_1^{\alpha_1}p_2^{\alpha_2}\cdots p_k^{\alpha_k}\), where \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\) are distinct primes and \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k\) are positive integers.

\[ \begin{aligned} f(N) &=p_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots p_k^{\alpha_k} \left(1-\frac1{p_1}\right)\cdots \left(1-\frac1{p_k}\right)\\ &=p_1^{\alpha_1-1}(p_1-1)\cdots p_k^{\alpha_k-1}(p_k-1) \end{aligned} \]

Thus \(\displaystyle f(N)\in\mathbb Z\)

(b)(i) \(\quad \boxed{\text{False}}\)

\[ f(3)=2,\quad f(9)=6, \quad f(27)=18 \]
\[ f(3)f(9)=12\neq18=f(27) \]

(b)(ii) \(\quad \boxed{\text{True}}\)

\[ \begin{aligned} f(p)f(q) &=p\left(1-\frac1p\right) q\left(1-\frac1q\right)\\ &=pq\left(1-\frac1p\right)\left(1-\frac1q\right)\\ &=f(pq) \end{aligned} \]

(b)(iii) \(\quad \boxed{\text{False}}\)

\[ f(1)=1,\qquad f(3)=2 \]
\[ f(1)f(3)=2=f(3)=f(1\cdot3) \]

But \(1\) is not prime

\[ \because 146410=10\cdot11^4 \]
\[ \therefore p-1=10,\qquad p^{m-1}=11^4 \]
\[ \boxed{p=11,\qquad m=5} \]
