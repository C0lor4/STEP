\textbf{(i)}

$$y=x^4-6x^2+9=(x^2-3)^2$$

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=4x^3-12x=4x(x^2-3)$$

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=0\implies x=0,\ \pm\sqrt3$$

$$y(0)=9,\qquad y(\pm\sqrt3)=0$$

Stationary points are

$$(-\sqrt3,0),\quad (0,9),\quad (\sqrt3,0)$$

\textbf{(ii)} Let $\ds \mathrm{f}_a(x)=x^4-6x^2+ax+b$

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=4x^3-12x+a,\qquad \frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=12x^2-12$$

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=0\implies x=\pm1$$

When $\ds x=1,\ 4-12+a=0\implies a=8$

When $\ds x=-1, \ -4+12+a=0\implies a=-8$

When $a=8$,

$$y=x^4-6x^2+8x+b,\qquad \frac{dy}{dx}=4(x-1)^2(x+2)$$

$$\frac{dy}{dx}<0\quad (x<-2),\qquad \frac{dy}{dx}\geq 0\quad(x>-2)$$

Minimum point has $x=-2,\ y(-2)=b-24$

$$
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{array}{c!{\vrule width 1.2pt}c|c|c}
b & b>24 & b=24 & b<24\\
\hline
\rule[-4pt]{0pt}{14pt}
\text{roots} & 0 & 1 & 2
\end{array}
$$

$\because\ \ds \mathrm{f}_{-8}(x)=x^4-6x^2-8x+b=\mathrm{f}_{8}(-x)$

$\therefore$ The roots count is the same for $\ds a=-8$

\textbf{(iii)}

$$y=x^4-6x^2+ax,\qquad a>8$$

$$\frac{dy}{dx}=4x^3-12x+a$$

Let $\ds \mathrm{g}(x)=4x^3-12x+a$

$$\mathrm{g}'(x)=12x^2-12$$

$$\mathrm{g}(-1)=a+8>0,\qquad \mathrm{g}(1)=a-8>0$$

$$\mathrm{g}(-2)=a-8>0,\qquad \lim_{x\to-\infty}\mathrm{g}(x)=-\infty$$

There is one stationary point at $x=\alpha$, where $\alpha<-2$

$$4\alpha^3-12\alpha+a=0$$

$$a=12\alpha-4\alpha^3$$

$$y(\alpha)=\alpha^4-6\alpha^2+a\alpha
=3\alpha^2(2-\alpha^2)<0$$

$\ds\frac{dy}{dx}<0$ for $x<\alpha$ and $\ds\frac{dy}{dx}>0$ for $x>\alpha$, so $y=0$ has exactly two roots.

$$y=x(x^3-6x+a)$$

Let $\beta$ be the negative root of $\ds x^3-6x+a=0$

$$\beta<\alpha<-2,\qquad y=0\text{ at }x=\beta,0$$

$\ds\mathrm{g}$ has a minimum value $a-8$ at $x=1$, so the curve is close to flat near $x=1$ when $a$ is close to $8$.
